You're probably cheating your chest supported rows without even realizing it.
Here's the mistake I see all the time.
Lifters initiate the rep by rocking backward, extending their spine and then lifting their
chest off the pad at the top because they think they're getting more range of motion,
but they're not.
Just because the handles traveled further doesn't mean your upper back went through
more productive range of motion.
So try this instead.
Drop the weight about 25% and scoot your hips back a few inches so you have a slight forward
lean into the pad.
Now here's the key.
Initiate the movement by simultaneously driving your chest into the pad as you drive your
elbows back.
Think opposing forces.
Chest forward, elbows backward.
And at the top, keep your chest in contact with the pad.
We'll eliminate the momentum, keep tension where you actually want it, your upper back,
and stop manufacturing extra range of motion by extending your spine.
So check your ego, lighten the weight, row instead of rock, and follow me.
